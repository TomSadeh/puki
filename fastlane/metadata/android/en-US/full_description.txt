Puki is a gentle game world for toddlers (about 2 to 4), in Hebrew and English, with a friendly parrot who cheers them on.

FREE, FOREVER
No price, no in-app purchases, no subscriptions, no "full version" and no ads. Nothing ever stops the fun to ask for money. Puki is open source (MIT), made by a parent who got tired of toddler games that do.

THE GAMES
• Animal Garden: feed each animal the right food
• Colors, Shapes, Counting and Letters, with spoken prompts
• Music: drum, xylophone, tambourine and maracas
• My Day: bath, meals, getting dressed, tidying up, brushing teeth and bedtime
• Drawing on an easel, and a sticker album that fills up with stars
• Peekaboo barn: find the hiding animal by its sound
• Echo Puki: say anything and Puki says it back in a silly parrot voice
• Say it with Puki: a picture, a word, and the child says it back

MADE FOR TODDLERS
No reading needed, big tap targets, and no failing: a wrong answer gets a gentle shake and "let's try again". Hebrew instructions match the child (girl or boy). When the time limit the parents set is up, there's no countdown: evening falls, Puki yawns, waves goodbye, and the music fades.

PUKI CAN TALK (OPTIONAL)
With a free program on the family's own PC, Puki comments on what the child does, listens when the child talks to him, looks at drawings and tells a short bedtime story. Everything runs on that PC at home; nothing goes to the cloud. Some high-end phones can run Puki's brain on the phone itself. Without either, Puki uses his recorded voice and every game works.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. The child's voice and drawings go only to the family's own PC or stay on the device. Settings sit behind a parent gate.

Source code and the PC program: https://github.com/TomSadeh/puki
